Strength

Strength, read set by set.

COROS strength sessions and your Hevy workouts in one place, on the muscles they really worked, and scored by what you lifted rather than by your heart rate. Made for anyone who runs, rides or hikes and lifts in the same week.

Why it needed work

COROS reads lifting poorly.

A COROS watch records your sets well. What happens to them afterwards is the problem, and it matters most when strength sits beside an endurance sport, because the lifting is what goes missing.

  • A load read from heart rate

    COROS scores every session by heart rate. Lifting raises it in short bursts, so an hour of heavy squats can count for less than a short jog, and your week's load, fitness and recommended range all under-count the work.

  • Movements on the wrong muscles

    COROS's catalogue files a squat as hamstring work, battle ropes as biceps, and every twist and side plank with no obliques at all. Your totals by muscle, and anything that reads them, inherit the mistake.

  • Half the lifting in another app

    Plenty of lifters log in Hevy, where the weights and reps are easier to enter. COROS never sees those workouts, so its picture of your week has a hole in it.

The Strength screen

Every set, on the muscles it worked.

One session at a time, or the weeks around it, beside a 3D figure lit by what each muscle did. Turn the figure, pick a muscle and see its training.

  • Sets, Volume or Load on the figure, and every muscle ranked by it.
  • Muscle coverage: how many muscle groups the period trained, and which it left out.
  • Records written in the exercise rows they were set in, and your main lifts across the window.
  • Weekly sets, so a block's build-up and its deload are plain to see.
  • Each set's effort and load, with Heracles TL chosen: how many reps it had left and what it added.
  • Honest about gaps: sets COROS filed only as Full Body are left off the map, and the screen says how many.

Exercises Library

Every movement, described again.

The app reads a movement by what it is, never by its name, and describes each one again, its muscles and equipment one by one: the obliques for twists and side planks, the neck for neck work, a squat on the quads and glutes, a battle rope on the shoulders. Where it disagrees with COROS, that is on purpose, and your totals and Coach read the right muscles.

Heracles Training Load

A training load that counts lifting.

Heracles Training Load scores a strength session from its sets: the weight, the reps, your body weight and the most you have lifted on each exercise. It works out how close to failure each set went, and weighs a minute of lifting as a steady minute at the same effort weighs. So a hard gym session lands on the same scale as COROS's load for a run, and your week adds up to what you actually did.

One formula for everyone, built from published research, with nothing tuned on your own data. COROS's figure stays the floor: a session never scores less than COROS gave it.

How Heracles Training Load is worked out

Turn it on

On the Strength screen, press Settings and, under Training load formula, choose Heracles TL over COROS TL. Each set then shows its predicted effort and load, and the body map gains Load.

Where it counts

  • Overview the week's load and its recommended range, Load Impact, Base Fitness, Intensity Trend, Coming up and the load heatmap
  • Calendar every strength session, done or planned, and each week's load against its target
  • Training Library the planned load of a plan's strength sessions, its weeks and the whole plan
  • Activities each session's load and the totals of a period
  • Coach and AI Plan how Coach weighs your lifting against your other sport, and the weekly load it plans to

Other sports keep COROS's load. Recovery and training effect stay COROS's, as on your watch. Where a figure differs from the COROS app, hovering it shows COROS's own.

Ask Coach about a session.

Coach reads every set, its weight and reps, and with Heracles TL chosen the load of each session. Ask whether last week's lifting left room for Sunday's long run, and the answer weighs both.
